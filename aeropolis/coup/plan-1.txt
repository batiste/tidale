# Aeropolis — Core Prototype ("Coup")

**Status: parked (2026-10-11).** Playable (play.html, vs bots) and simulated (sim.js), but less fun than the reference (../index.html). See Post-mortem at the end.

New prototype in a series. The reference version is ../index.html.
This directory explores a quite different version of the game.

2–4 players. Card draft: cards.json (20 Citizen, 20 Outcast, 20 Secrets, 10 characters, 3 stack cards).

## Concept

A city is on the edge of revolution.

Each player controls two characters (2 cards):
- **Citizen** (C) — High City
- **Outcast** (O) — Low City

The two character cards are stacked vertically. Each starts a horizontal **line** to its right.
Each character card has a unique effect. All cards are bridge sized.

Players place agents, gather Secrets and gain leverage over the city's personalities
to control locations when the Revolution comes.

**Core hypothesis: a coup needs an inside man.**
The Outcast brings force, the Citizen brings legitimacy. Neither controls a location alone.

---

## Terms

- **Agent**: a player's cube, placed on a location half. Not spent.
- **High / Low**: the two halves of each location. Citizen places agents in High, Outcast in Low.
- **Control**: see Locations.
- **Line**: the cards to the right of a character. Max 2 cards; adding a 3rd discards the oldest.
- **Arms slot**: on about 50% of cards, with a location affinity (see Arms).
- **Cell**: a Citizen card and an Outcast card in the same column whose arms slots name the same location: +1 strength there while both stay.
- **Pay → get**: an optional effect, resolved each time it fires (e.g. pay 1 gold → 1 agent), after the line's other effects. Agent placement at a location of your choice costs 1 gold (or 1 arm); agents at a fixed location are free.
- **Power cards**: 10–20% of market cards carry an expensive pay → get (2–3 resources, mixed gold/arms/Secrets) for a strong effect, e.g. several agents or removing opponent agents.
- **Personality**: a token tied to a location, target of leverage (see Secrets).
- **Resources**: gold, arms. Both pay card costs. Some cards also cost a Secret (discard it unrevealed): a design space to explore.

---

## Split economy

- Outcast line: mostly Secrets, arms, Low agents.
- Citizen line: mostly gold, High agents.

Each side needs what the other produces.

**Market rates** (cards.json `_schema.values`): 1 arm ≈ 1.5 gold, 1 Secret ≈ 2 gold, 1 agent ≈ 1.5 gold (anywhere) or 1 gold (fixed location).
Baseline only: the stack cards trade at market rate; a market card or character earns its place with one preferential conversion (a better deal in one direction). `node sim.js audit` shows each card's premium.

---

## Setup

- Each player: 4 gold, 1 arm, 12 agents in reserve (shared by Citizen and Outcast). No agents on the board.
- Market: 2 Citizen + 2 Outcast cards, each refilled from its own deck, plus 3 free stacks always available: **Stipend** (Citizen, +1 gold), **Retainer** (Citizen, pay 1 gold → 1 High agent), **Hired Muscle** (Outcast, pay 1 gold → 1 Low agent).
- Revolution track at 0.
- First player: random; passes left each round.
- Empty deck (market or Secrets): shuffle its discards into it.

---

## Actions

Each round, players take 3 turns each, one at a time, going around the table 3 times.
On a turn, take 1 action:

**Investigate**: draw 1 Secret.

**Build connection**: take 1 card from the market, pay its cost (gold, arms or free).
Add it to the line of its own character (C cards to C, O cards to O).
Activate that line: resolve every effect on it, character included (as in ../index.html).
Refill the market. Stack cards take a line slot like any other.

Agents are placed only by effects (cards, Secrets, Workers); with an empty reserve, a placement moves one of your agents (same half) instead.
**Move**: an agent changes location, staying in its half.

Secrets are used for free, at any time (see Secrets).

---

## Locations

Each location has a High half and a Low half.

**Control**: the player with the highest strength, among players with at least 1 agent in High.
Strength = your agents in High + your agents in Low + your arms on cards with that location's affinity + your cells there.
Cap: at most 3 of your Low agents per location (a placement beyond it does nothing).
Tie: nobody.

| Location    | Personality | Reward             | Revolution VP |
|-------------|-------------|--------------------|---------------|
| Governor    | Governor    | draw a Secret      | 3             |
| Military    | General     | 1 arm              | 3             |
| Guild       | Guildmaster | 3 gold             | 3             |
| Underground | Kingpin     | your Outcast line holds 3 cards until the next control (losing it: discard the oldest) | 3 |
| Printer     | —           | draw 2 Secrets, discard 1 | 1 |
| Workers     | —           | place 1 agent (High or Low) | 1 |

**Seats of power**: the 4 locations with a personality are worth 3 VP; Printer and Workers 1 VP.
Leverage over a personality therefore matters where it counts most.

---

## Arms

Arms come from line effects and Military. They also pay card costs.

Arming a card: put 1 arm from your supply on its arms slot.
- When you add a card with an arms slot to a line, you may arm it (before the line activates).
- At the end of the round, you may put 1 arm on one card in your lines.

A card leaving the line discards its arms.

Converting slots: the arm is spent at once for the slot's effect (gold or Secret).

---

## End of round

1. Arm one card (see Arms).
2. Resolve Control of every location; each controller takes its reward.
3. Discard all arms on cards.
4. Upkeep: each controller sends 1 of their Low agents there back to reserve.
5. Revolution +1.

In the last round, the Revolution replaces steps 2–5.

---

## Secrets

One shared Secret deck. Hand limit 3: discard down to 3 after drawing. Each Secret has:
- a title
- a **Reveal** effect (weak)
- a **Prepared** effect (strong, at the Revolution)
- optionally a personality

At any time, for free, a Secret can be:
- **Exploited**: reveal it, apply Reveal, discard it.
- **Leveraged** (personality Secrets only): discard it to blackmail that personality:
  - win a contract: take a market card for free (as Build connection, without cost), or
  - remove an opponent's agent (either half) from the personality's location.

Otherwise it stays hidden as **Prepared** until the Revolution.

At most 10–15% of Secrets score VP (under a condition).

Example: **MILITARY PLANS** (General)
- Reveal: place 2 agents in Military Low.
- Prepared: place 2 agents in Military Low and 1 in High.

---

## Revolution

Revolution track: a countdown, +1 at the end of each round. The game lasts 6 rounds.
At the end of round 6 (see End of round):
1. Reveal and resolve all Prepared Secrets, in turn order.
2. Resolve Control of every location (arms on cards count).
3. Score: Revolution VP of each controlled location, plus Secret VP.

Tie: most gold left, then most arms.

The Revolution cannot be prevented, only prepared for.

Later: a Revolution catalyst players benefit from triggering.

## Prototype goal

- Does the Citizen/Outcast duality feel real?
- Does the High/Low control rule make the two characters work together?
- Does the theme (intrigue, secrets, blackmail) match the mechanics?

---

## Post-mortem

**What did not work** (playtest vs the reference):
- 2 lines, one per character, are a weaker puzzle than the reference's 4 lanes (tuck above / below).
- Stack cards were a crutch for the missing action economy; they made turns samey.
- Location control needed patch after patch (Low cap, upkeep, empty-reserve move, tie-break, cells, pay costs): the core loop was not carrying the game.
- No round climax: end of round is bookkeeping; the reference's Uprising is something to aspire to.
- Secrets were hidden but not interactive; in practice a free bonus effect. Blackmail created little tension.

**Worth keeping:**
- Fewer resource types: gold, arms and Secrets (vs Papers, Secrets, Stims and Arms in the reference). Easier to read and to price, and every card conversion is legible.
- Tooling: search bot with logged reasoning, simulator (play / compare / balance / audit), value model (market rates).
- Market rates as a design baseline; cards earn their place with one preferential conversion.
- Power cards: 10–20% of the deck, an expensive mixed-resource pay → get.
- "A coup needs both sides": possibly as an Uprising condition in the reference rather than a whole game.
