# Aeropolis — Bribes & Grievances (draft 1)

Superseded by plan-2.txt (the Spire as a multiplier). Kept for history.

New prototype in the series. Overview of all versions: ../index.html.
Aim: Knizia-simple. One mechanism (an auction), one track (the Spire), few components.

3–5 players. 20–30 minutes.

## Concept

The city's districts are being sold off. Each player has a Citizen high on the Spire and an Outcast at its base.
You buy districts with your Citizen's standing: it sinks. When you lose an auction, your Outcast's grievance grows: it rises.
Every auction pushes everyone toward the meeting point. When any player's two characters meet, the revolution breaks out
and the game ends.

The Spire is the money, the score and the clock at the same time.

---

## Components

- Spire track: heights 1–15.
- Per player: 2 pawns (Citizen, Outcast).
- 30 district cards: 5 factions (Crown, Church, Guilds, Commune, Underworld) × 6, values −1, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2.
- 4 special cards (see Specials).
- Auctioneer token.

---

## Setup

- Citizens on 15, Outcasts on 1.
- Shuffle districts and specials into one deck.
- Random first auctioneer.

---

## Auction (one per turn)

1. The auctioneer reveals the top card.
2. Once around, starting left of the auctioneer and ending with the auctioneer: each player passes or bids higher
   than the current bid. One bid each.
3. The highest bidder takes the card face up and sinks their Citizen by the bid.
4. Every player who bid and lost: Outcast rises 1 (grievance). Passing earns nothing.
5. Nobody bids: the district burns. Every Outcast rises 1.
6. The auctioneer token passes left.

You may bid an amount that makes your characters meet.

---

## End

- After an auction, if any player's Outcast is level with or above their Citizen, the revolution breaks out.
  The current round is finished (auctions continue until the first auctioneer would start again), then the game ends.
  So nobody can end the game with one bid or one pass: everyone gets an answer.
- Deck empty: the game ends.

## Score

- Your Citizen's height + your Outcast's height.
- + the value of your districts.
- + 3 for each faction where you hold the most cards. Tie: nobody.

Highest total wins. Tie: highest Citizen.

So a bid costs points, a lost bid pays 1 point, and every move on the Spire brings the end closer.

---

## Specials (4 cards, to test one at a time)

- Riot: no auction. Every Outcast rises 1.
- Double: reveal 2 more cards; they are auctioned together.
- Closed fist: everyone bids at once, in secret. Highest wins; tie: nobody, the district burns.
- Amnesty: auctioned like a district; the winner may move their Outcast down 2 (delay the end).

---

## Numbers (to check with the sim)

- Gap between a player's characters at the start: 14.
- With 4 players and bids around 3, each auction moves the Spire about 6 steps in total, so 1.5 per player.
- First meeting after about 9–10 auctions: a third of the deck is never seen, so every game differs.

---

## Open questions

- Bidding: once around (above), or open free-for-all bidding (Modern Art)? Once around is faster and favours the auctioneer.
- Grievance: settled for now on "only those who bid and lost". With +1 for every loser, passing was free income:
  the Spire made up 70% of every score and buying rarely beat passing.
- Majority bonus: flat 3, or the number of cards of that faction sold in the game (values change during play)?
- Should the player who triggers the revolution gain or lose something (e.g. lose their majority bonuses)?
- Outcast height in the score: keeps losing auctions worthwhile; does it make passing too comfortable?

---

## Prototype goals

- Does bidding with your own standing create the hard choices of Lost Cities / Modern Art?
- Is timing the revolution a real decision (bid big to end while ahead)?
- Do the two characters feel tied together, with one side paying and the other side growing?
