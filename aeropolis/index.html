Aeropolis

A city on the edge of revolution. Every prototype shares one idea: each player plays a Citizen descending the Spire and an Outcast climbing it. Here is every version so far, what it tries, and what we learned.

Aeropolis

Reference

Take a card and tuck it above or below one of your characters: that side activates, every card in it. Send troops into the city, win Uprisings, hold key locations to make your Outcast climb. Score both heights added together.

RulesPlaySelf-assessment

Works

  • Tucking cards above or below, as you choose.
  • Fighting over the five locations.
  • The Spire as both resource and score.

Does not

  • No real interaction between your two characters (blackmail, smuggling…).
  • Weak interaction between players; little sense of intrigue.
  • Too many resources.
  • Your two characters meeting on the Spire rarely matters.

Banners

In progress

No cards: move a character to a location, place influence, take its action; same column as your other character: a tandem bonus. Five factions fly banners over the city; you score influence under your banners and the locations you control. Every action costs Spire time; when your characters meet you score and are out.

RulesPlayCards

Works

  • Strong identity: banners, factions, card art.
  • Time on the Spire as the only cost; scoring when you go out makes the ending fair.
  • Control scoring (3 per location, any banner) calmed the banner wars.
  • Hot-seat with bots, simulator, readable logs.

Does not

  • Too many exceptions: 10 character powers, 8 actions, 4 tandems.
  • The Outcast plays a helper role (about a quarter of the time spent).
  • Removal actions (Purge, Riot) are almost never worth a turn.
  • The hidden allegiance (intrigue) was cut: not interesting in play.

Banners: Simple

Tried

Banners cut to the bone, in the spirit of Knizia: move, then place influence or raise your banner where you control; tandem lets your other character act too. You score only your weaker banner.

RulesPlay

Works

  • Rules fit on one page, no exceptions.
  • Fair timing: going out first or last does not decide the game.

Does not

  • Not interesting: almost every turn is "place where I already lead".
  • Players rarely get in each other's way; banners barely change.
  • Exploit: park the Citizen, shuttle the Outcast under it for cheap tandems.

Coup

Parked

A card draft: each character has a line of cards, with Secrets and stack cards; location control by placement, blackmail between your characters.

PlayPlan & post-mortem

Worth keeping

  • Fewer resources (gold, arms, Secrets): easy to read and to price.
  • Tooling: search bot with reasoning, simulator, market-rate value model.
  • "A coup needs both sides" as a possible Uprising condition.

Does not

  • Two lines (one per character) are a weaker puzzle than tucking above or below.
  • Location control needed patch after patch; stack cards made turns samey.
  • No round climax; Secrets were a free bonus, not interaction.

Double Life

Draft

A lighter spin-off: draft actions from one shared row to place cubes in the city. Control a location to climb the Spire with the side it feeds. You score only your weaker side.

Undercity

Draft

Hand-building, no rounds: play one card per turn to push cubes around the city. When your hand is empty, regroup: score the districts you control and take your cards back. Buying cards brings your Citizen down toward your Outcast.

Spire

Draft

Hand management on a hex tower: every card is an action or coin. Cash cards to move a pawn; where it stops, its hex activates. When your pawns meet, you score the height of your lower one.

Deck-builder

First draft

The first version: build a deck from a starter set and the Noodle Shop, play cards into your two characters' lines, slot cubes from the city, bid on Conflicts, trigger the Revolution.

ConceptRulesBoardCardsPlay

Works

  • Conflict bidding: real, scarce, zero-sum interaction.
  • The Citizen/Outcast split as the core hook.

Does not

  • Abundance: a full hand can usually all be played, so turns are bookkeeping.
  • The Spire race only matters at the Revolution and the final scoring.
